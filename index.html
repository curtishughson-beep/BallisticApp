Read this before you trust the numbers

Published data, not your rifle. Every muzzle velocity and ballistic coefficient here is the manufacturer’s figure, taken from a 24 in test barrel. Real rifles routinely run 50–100 fps off the box. The solver is only as good as the velocity you feed it — a chronograph is the single best upgrade to this app’s accuracy, and there is a field for it in the rifle panel.

Verify at distance. This is a point-mass solver with a G1/G7 drag model, humid-air density correction, and a full crosswind integration. It does not model spin drift, aerodynamic jump, Coriolis, or the shot angle up or down a coulee. Past roughly 400 yd those start to matter. Print the dope table, then confirm it on paper at the ranges you actually intend to shoot.

Moose thresholds. The 1500 ft·lb energy floor is the widely used guideline for moose, not a law. Terminal performance is the harder limit: a monolithic like a TTSX or CX wants roughly 2000 fps at impact to open, cup-and-core bullets around 1800, and bonded or ELD-X style bullets around 1600. The “max range” column takes the shorter of those two. Shot placement outranks all of it.

Manitoba. Moose seasons here are draw-based across most game hunting areas and several GHAs are closed to moose hunting outright under conservation closures. Confirm your GHA and licence in the current Manitoba Hunting Guide before you plan around any of this.
